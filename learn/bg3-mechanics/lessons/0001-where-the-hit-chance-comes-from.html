Where the hit chance comes from

BG3 shows a percentage for each weapon attack and each spell attack. That percentage comes from one d20 roll plus a bonus, compared against a number. This lesson gives you the formula. Then you apply the formula until you can predict the tooltip yourself.

The one formula

d20 + ability modifier + proficiency bonus  ≥  target's Armour Class → hit

The formula is complete. The wiki states it as "Result = d20 + Ability score modifier + Proficiency bonus". A hit happens when the result equals or exceeds the Armour Class. Source: bg3.wiki, Attack roll.

Your Sorcerer is level 2 and has 16 Charisma. The ability modifier is +3 and the proficiency bonus is +2. The spell attack bonus is therefore +5. Gale has 17 Intelligence, so his bonus is also +5. Proficiency by level: bg3.wiki, Sorcerer.

Two faces of the die ignore the formula. A natural 20 always hits and is a critical hit. A natural 1 always misses. Source: bg3.wiki, Attack roll.

Conversion of the formula to a percentage

Subtract your bonus from the Armour Class of the target. The result is the lowest die face that hits. Count the faces from that number to 20. Then divide the count by 20.

Example: the bonus is +5 and the enemy has AC 13. You need 13 − 5 = 8 or higher. Faces 8 to 20 are 13 faces. 13 ÷ 20 = 65 percent. The tooltip shows this number.

Use the calculator. Set the AC to 18. The number falls to 40 percent. Then set the bonus to 7. This shows the effect of one more point of Charisma and one more point of proficiency. Each +1 adds exactly one face on the die, which is 5 percentage points. The natural 1 and natural 20 limits stop this pattern.

The effect of advantage

Advantage means that the game rolls two d20s and keeps the higher die. Disadvantage keeps the lower die. Nothing else changes: the bonus and the AC stay the same. Source: bg3.wiki, Advantage.

The percentage changes a lot, because only one of the two dice must reach the target number. A flat roll that hits 65 percent of the time misses 35 percent of the time. With advantage you miss only when both dice miss: 0.35 × 0.35 = 12 percent. Therefore you hit 88 percent of the time. With disadvantage you hit only when both dice hit: 0.65 × 0.65 = 42 percent.

Two rules cause frequent errors. Both rules come directly from the wiki:

  • Sources never stack. Three reasons for advantage still give two dice. "Creatures receive no additional benefit or penalty from having multiple sources of advantage or disadvantage."
  • One of each cancels everything. Two sources of advantage and one source of disadvantage give a flat roll. "Creatures that have both advantage and disadvantage on a roll only roll a single die, even if they have multiple sources of either."

Set the calculator above to Advantage and then to Disadvantage. Compare the results at different ACs. The change is largest in the middle of the range. At 95 percent or 5 percent the change is very small.

A correction: high ground is not advantage

Many guides say that high ground gives advantage. I gave you this same wrong information earlier in our conversation. The released game does not work in this way. An attack from at least 2.5 m above the target gives a flat +2 to the attack roll. An attack from below gives −2. Source: bg3.wiki, High ground rules.

This rule gives you a better result. The bonus is flat and not advantage. Therefore it stacks with real advantage from a prone target or a blinded target. High ground plus a knocked-down enemy gives +2 and two dice.

Practice: the tooltip rebuild

Do this task in your next fight, before you read more. Hover over an attack and note the percentage. Roll the attack. Then open the combat log with the scroll icon at the bottom right. Find the line for that attack. The line lists the die, each modifier by name, and the AC. Confirm that the percentage matches the formula.
