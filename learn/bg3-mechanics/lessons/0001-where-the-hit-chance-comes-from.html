Where the hit chance comes from

Every percentage BG3 shows you for a weapon attack or a spell attack is one d20 roll plus a bonus, compared against a number. This lesson gives you the formula, then makes you use it until the tooltip stops being magic.

The one formula

d20 + ability modifier + proficiency bonus  ≥  target's Armour Class → hit

That is the whole thing. The wiki states it as "Result = d20 + Ability score modifier + Proficiency bonus" and a hit happens when the result equals or exceeds AC. Source: bg3.wiki, Attack roll.

For your Sorcerer at level 2 with 16 Charisma, the ability modifier is +3 and the proficiency bonus is +2, so your spell attack bonus is +5. For Gale with 17 Intelligence it is the same +5. Proficiency by level: bg3.wiki, Sorcerer.

Two faces of the die ignore the math. A natural 20 always hits and is a critical hit. A natural 1 always misses. Source: bg3.wiki, Attack roll.

Turning it into a percentage

Subtract your bonus from the target's AC. That is the lowest die face that hits. Count how many faces from that number up to 20, then divide by 20.

Example: bonus +5, enemy AC 13. You need 13 − 5 = 8 or higher. Faces 8 through 20 is 13 faces. 13 ÷ 20 = 65%. That is the number the tooltip shows.

Try it: set AC to 18 and watch the number fall to 40%. Then set your bonus to 7 and see what one point of Charisma plus one point of proficiency buys you. Each +1 is exactly one more face on the die, which is 5 percentage points, until you run into the natural 1 and natural 20 floors.

What advantage really does

Advantage means roll two d20s and keep the higher. Disadvantage keeps the lower. Nothing else changes: same bonus, same AC. Source: bg3.wiki, Advantage.

The percentage moves a lot because you only need one of two dice to clear the bar. If a flat roll hits 65% of the time, it misses 35% of the time. With advantage you miss only when both dice miss: 0.35 × 0.35 = 12%, so you hit 88%. With disadvantage you hit only when both hit: 0.65 × 0.65 = 42%.

Two rules that trip people up, both straight from the wiki:

  • Sources never stack. Three reasons for advantage still means two dice. "Creatures receive no additional benefit or penalty from having multiple sources of advantage or disadvantage."
  • One of each cancels everything. Two sources of advantage and one of disadvantage is a flat roll. "Creatures that have both advantage and disadvantage on a roll only roll a single die, even if they have multiple sources of either."

Switch the calculator above to Advantage and Disadvantage to feel the size of the swing at different ACs. It matters most in the middle. At 95% or 5% it barely moves the needle.

A correction: high ground is not advantage

Many guides, and I earlier in our conversation, say high ground gives advantage. In the released game it does not. Attacking from at least 2.5 m above the target gives a flat +2 to the attack roll, and attacking from below gives −2. Source: bg3.wiki, High ground rules.

This is actually better for you. Because it is a flat bonus and not advantage, it stacks with real advantage from a prone or blinded target. High ground plus a knocked-down enemy is +2 and two dice.

Practice: rebuild the tooltip

Do this in your next fight, before reading further. Hover an attack, note the percentage, then open the combat log after you roll (the scroll icon, bottom right). Find the line for that attack. It lists the die, each modifier by name, and the AC. Check that the percentage you saw matches the formula.
