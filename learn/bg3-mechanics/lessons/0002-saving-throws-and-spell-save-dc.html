Saving throws and Spell Save DC

Lesson 1 covered the rolls that you make. This lesson covers the rolls that the enemy makes. Most of your spells never roll an attack. Instead the target rolls a saving throw against one fixed number. That number is your Spell Save DC. This lesson teaches you to compute the DC, to read the save of the target, and to choose the spell that the target is least able to resist.

Two kinds of harmful spell

BG3 splits harmful spells into two groups. Spells in the form of a ray or a projectile need an attack roll against the Armour Class of the target. All other harmful spells let the target attempt a saving throw. Source: bg3.wiki, Spellcasting.

Lesson 1 gave you the first group. This lesson gives you the second group. Read the spell tooltip and find the words "Save" or "Saving Throw". Those words tell you which group the spell is in.

The one formula

Spell Save DC = 8 + proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier
target: d20 + ability modifier + proficiency bonus (if proficient)  ≥  DC → the target saves

The target succeeds when the result equals or exceeds your Spell Save DC. Source: bg3.wiki, Saving throw.

Your Sorcerer casts with Charisma and has 16 Charisma, so the ability modifier is +3. At level 2 the proficiency bonus is +2. Therefore your Spell Save DC is 8 + 2 + 3 = 13. Gale casts with Intelligence and has 17 Intelligence, so his modifier is also +3 and his DC is also 13. Spellcasting ability by class: bg3.wiki, Spellcasting. Proficiency by level: bg3.wiki, Sorcerer.

At character level 5 the proficiency bonus rises to +3. Your DC then rises to 14 without any change to Charisma. Source: bg3.wiki, Sorcerer.

One important difference from lesson 1: a saving throw has no automatic result. A natural 20 does not grant an automatic success and a natural 1 does not force an automatic failure. The only exceptions are concentration saves and dialogue saves. Source: bg3.wiki, Saving throw.

Conversion of the DC to a percentage

Subtract the save bonus of the target from your Spell Save DC. The result is the lowest die face on which the target succeeds. Count the faces from that number to 20. Then divide the count by 20. That fraction is the chance of a save. The rest of the die is your chance to land the spell.

Example: your DC is 13 and the target has a Wisdom save bonus of −1. The target needs 13 − (−1) = 14 or higher. Faces 14 to 20 are 7 faces. 7 ÷ 20 = 35 percent for the save. Therefore the spell lands 65 percent of the time.

Use the calculator. Set the save bonus to +3, which is a common value for a strong save. The chance to land falls to 45 percent. Then set the DC to 14 for level 5. The chance rises to 50 percent. Each point of difference between the DC and the save bonus is one face on the die, which is 5 percentage points.

Advantage and disadvantage on saves

Advantage and disadvantage apply to saving throws in the same way as to attack rolls. The wiki states that "Rolling two dice only applies to d20 rolls: Attack Rolls, Saving Throws, and Ability Checks." The same two rules from lesson 1 apply. Multiple sources do not stack, and one source of each cancels to a single flat die. Source: bg3.wiki, Advantage.

Disadvantage on a save is strong, because the target must clear the DC on both dice. Return to the example. A flat save of 35 percent becomes 0.35 × 0.35 = 12.25 percent with disadvantage. Your chance to land the spell rises from 65 percent to 87.8 percent. Set the calculator to "Target has disadvantage" and confirm this result.

Your class buys this effect directly. The Heightened Spell Metamagic costs 3 sorcery points and gives the target disadvantage on its first saving throw. Your Sorcerer gains access to this option at class level 3. Source: bg3.wiki, Sorcerer.

The Examine panel

Do not guess the save of a target. Read it. Hover over the creature and press T. The wiki states that "A target's saving throws can be checked by examining them (shortcut 'T')." Source: bg3.wiki, Abilities. The panel also lists resistances and current conditions. Source: Prima Games, How to examine enemy weaknesses and resistances.

The panel gives the ability score and the save number together. Convert a score to a modifier in this way: subtract 10 from the score, divide by 2, and round down. A score of 8 gives −1. A score of 12 gives +1. A score of 19 gives +4. Source: bg3.wiki, Abilities.

Warning: the save number can exceed the ability modifier, because a proficient creature adds its proficiency bonus. Source: bg3.wiki, Saving throw. Read the save number itself when the panel shows it, and use the ability modifier only as a fallback.

Choice of the weakest save

A low ability score gives a low save. Therefore the correct question before a cast is short: which of my spells targets the lowest number on this panel?

Two patterns help you to make a first guess. Large brutes usually have low Dexterity and low Wisdom. Nimble enemies usually have low Strength and low Wisdom. Treat both patterns as a guess and confirm the guess in the Examine panel. The following three stat blocks show the pattern on real enemies.

Read the three rows against your DC 13. Wisdom is the lowest save or an equal-lowest save in all three rows. A Wisdom spell against the Ogre Brute needs a 15 from the target, which is 6 faces, so the spell lands 70 percent of the time. A Constitution spell against the same target needs a 10, which is 11 faces, so that spell lands only 45 percent of the time. The same DC gives a difference of 25 percentage points.

Enemy player-class characters follow the class table instead. A Fighter is proficient in Strength and Constitution saves. A Rogue is proficient in Dexterity and Intelligence saves. Target the two abilities that the class table does not list. Full table: bg3.wiki, Saving throw, and the reference sheet.

Three points matter here. Sleep ignores this whole lesson, because it uses a hit point threshold and not a saving throw. Source: bg3.wiki, Sleep. Ray of Sickness needs both rolls: your attack roll against the Armour Class, and then a Constitution save against the Poisoned condition. Source: bg3.wiki, Ray of Sickness. Tasha's Hideous Laughter is on the Wizard list and not on the Sorcerer list, so it belongs to Gale. Source: bg3.wiki, Tasha's Hideous Laughter.

Note the second column of effects. A damage spell such as Burning Hands still deals half damage on a save. A condition spell such as Hold Person gives you nothing on a save. Therefore save the sorcery points for the condition spells, where a failure costs you the whole action.

Practice: the prediction before the cast

Do this task in your next fight. Choose one enemy and press T. Write down the six save numbers. Compute the chance for two spells with the formula: your DC minus the save bonus gives the needed face, and the faces below that number are your chance to land the spell. Cast the better spell. Then open the combat log and confirm the die roll, the modifier, and the DC against your numbers.
