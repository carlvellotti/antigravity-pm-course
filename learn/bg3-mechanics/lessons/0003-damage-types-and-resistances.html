Damage types and resistances

A hit that lands can still deal half damage, or no damage at all. The target decides this, not your roll. This lesson teaches you to read the resistances of the target before you cast, to pick a damage type that the target does not resist, and to create a vulnerability on purpose with water.

The 13 damage types

Baldur's Gate 3 has 13 damage types in three groups. Source: bg3.wiki, Damage types.

GroupTypes
PhysicalBludgeoning, Piercing, Slashing
ElementalAcid, Cold, Fire, Lightning, Poison, Thunder
OtherForce, Necrotic, Psychic, Radiant

Every damage instance in the game carries exactly one of these labels. The label decides which of the three modifiers below applies.

The three modifiers

resistance → damage ÷ 2, rounded down vulnerability → damage × 2 immunity → damage = 0

The wiki gives the resistance rule in one sentence: "Resistance means the total damage taken from a type of damage is halved (rounded down)." Source: bg3.wiki, Resistances.

Three rules control how the modifiers combine. Learn all three.

  • Multiple resistances to the same damage type do not stack. Two sources give the same half damage as one source.
  • Resistance and vulnerability to the same damage type cancel each other. The target then takes normal damage.
  • Immunity ignores both of the other modifiers. "Immunity means the total damage taken from a type of damage is reduced to 0. This means the creature takes no damage of that type, even if another effect applies vulnerability to the same type."

Sources: bg3.wiki, Damage types and bg3.wiki, Resistances.

Set the base damage to 9 and step through the five target states. Note two results. Immunity turns a good roll into nothing. "Resistant and vulnerable" returns the full 9, because the two modifiers cancel. Note also that the calculator rounds down. Resistance against 9 damage gives 4, not 4.5.

How to read the Examine panel

Hover the cursor over an enemy and press T. This opens the Examine panel. Default keybinding "Examine / Pin Tooltip": bg3.wiki, Options.

The panel shows one icon for each damage type. An arrow on an icon marks a modifier. The colour of the arrow tells you which damage sources the modifier covers. The wiki states it directly:

"resistance is indicated by an upwards blue and / or grey arrow on the corresponding element. Grey indicates that non-magical sources of damage are reduced, and blue indicates that magical sources of damage are reduced."

Source: bg3.wiki, Resistances.

Vulnerability uses a downward red arrow. Immunity uses a yellow outline around the shield of the damage type. A grey outline marks immunity to non-magical sources only. Source: bg3.wiki, Resistances.

The magical and non-magical split matters for you. Spells are magical sources of damage, and so are weapons of uncommon rarity and above. Source: bg3.wiki, Resistances. Your Sorcerer therefore reads the blue arrows. A grey arrow alone does not reduce your spell damage. It does reduce the damage from a common weapon in the hands of a melee companion.

Your flexible spell: Chromatic Orb

Chromatic Orb is a level 1 Evocation spell. The Sorcerer and the Wizard learn it at level 1. You choose the element when you cast it. The game lists a separate variant for each element: Acid, Cold, Fire, Lightning, Poison and Thunder. Source: bg3.wiki, Chromatic Orb.

The damage is not equal across the variants. Thunder deals 3d8. Acid, Cold, Fire, Lightning and Poison each deal 2d8 and create a surface. Source: bg3.wiki, Chromatic Orb. The Lightning variant creates an Electrified Water surface. Source: bg3.wiki, Chromatic Orb: Lightning. The Cold variant creates an Ice surface. Source: bg3.wiki, Chromatic Orb: Cold.

A note before you plan around it: many summaries give Chromatic Orb as 3d8 for every element. That is wrong. Only the Thunder variant deals 3d8. The five other variants deal 2d8 and add a surface. Treat the extra d8 on Thunder as the price of the surface.

Chromatic Orb needs an attack roll, so a resistance and a miss can both take the damage away. Magic Missile removes one of those two risks. It deals Force damage, it fires three darts of 1d4 + 1, and the darts always hit. Source: bg3.wiki, Magic Missile. Check the Force icon in the Examine panel. If that icon has no arrow, Magic Missile is your answer to an enemy that resists every element.

The Wet combination

You can create a vulnerability. The Wet condition changes the target as follows. Source: bg3.wiki, Wet.

  • The target becomes vulnerable to Lightning damage and Cold damage. Both types then deal double damage.
  • The target becomes resistant to Fire damage. Fire then deals half damage.
  • The target becomes immune to Burning and to Wild Magic: Burning.

Three more rules from the same page control the edge cases:

  • A Wet creature that already resists Lightning or Cold loses that resistance. It does not become vulnerable. "Wet entities who are resistant to Lightning or Cold damage have their resistances negated instead of becoming vulnerable."
  • A Chilled creature that becomes Wet turns into a Frozen creature. "If a already chilled creature is made wet, both conditions are replaced with Frozen."
  • Wet ends normal invisibility at once. "Becoming wet breaks normal invisibility immediately." Do not make your hidden Rogue Wet.

Warning: Wet also makes your own party vulnerable to Lightning and Cold. Place the water on the enemy, and keep your melee characters out of it.

How to apply Wet

Deep water and steam clouds apply Wet. The spell Create Water applies Wet. Source: bg3.wiki, Wet.

Create or Destroy Water is a level 1 Transmutation spell with a 4 m radius. The Cleric and the Druid learn it at level 1. A Conjuration School Wizard gets the Create Water variant at level 2, once per Short Rest and with no spell slot. A Storm Sorcery Sorcerer gets it at level 6. Source: bg3.wiki, Create or Destroy Water.

Read that list against your party. Your Sorcerer at level 2 does not have this spell. Gale is an Evocation Wizard, not a Conjuration Wizard, so Gale does not have it either. A Cleric companion or a Druid companion is your source of water. Send the water first, then cast the Lightning orb.

The math, one table

Chromatic Orb: Lightning deals 2d8. The average of 2d8 is 9. Chromatic Orb: Thunder deals 3d8, and the average of 3d8 is 13.5. Compare the outcomes.

CastTarget stateAverage beforeAverage after
Chromatic Orb: Lightning, 2d8Normal99
Chromatic Orb: Lightning, 2d8Wet, so vulnerable918
Chromatic Orb: Fire, 2d8Resistant to Fire94.25
Chromatic Orb: Thunder, 3d8Normal13.513.5
Magic Missile, 3 × (1d4 + 1)Normal10.510.5

One choice of element moves the result from 4.25 to 18. That is a factor of 4. No other decision on your turn at level 2 changes the damage by that much.

Why 4.25 and not 4.5? Resistance rounds down after each halving. Over all 64 results of 2d8, the rounding removes 0.25 damage on average. Halve first, then round down. Do not round the average.

Practice: one Examine, one element

Do this in your next fight. Complete all four steps before you cast.

  • Hover the first enemy and press T to open the Examine panel.
  • Read the damage type icons. Write down every blue arrow, every red arrow and every yellow outline.
  • Choose a Chromatic Orb element that has no arrow on it. If every element has an arrow, cast Magic Missile for Force damage.
  • If a Cleric or a Druid is in your party, cast Create Water on the enemy first, then cast Chromatic Orb: Lightning. Read the damage number in the combat log and confirm that it is double.
