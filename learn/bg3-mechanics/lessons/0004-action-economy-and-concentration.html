Action economy and concentration

Each turn gives you four resources. Most players spend one of them and end the turn. This lesson shows you how to spend all four every turn. It also shows you how to keep one concentration spell active for a complete fight.

The four resources of a turn

Every creature in the game holds the same set of resources. All of them recharge at the start of the turn of the creature. Source: bg3.wiki, Resources.

The wiki states the rule directly: "All creatures have 1 charge of each of the three resources that recharges at the beginning of each turn, but additional charges can be gained from various sources." Source: bg3.wiki, Actions.

An unspent resource is a loss. The resource does not carry over to the next turn. Therefore a turn with an unused bonus action is a turn at 75 percent power.

The action list and the bonus action list

Every creature has a set of common actions and a set of common bonus actions. Learn which list holds which ability. This knowledge decides how much you fit into one turn. Source: bg3.wiki, Actions.

BG3 differs from tabletop 5e here. In tabletop rules Shove costs an action and a jump is part of movement. In BG3 both Shove and Jump cost a bonus action. This difference is a gift. Your Sorcerer can cast a spell with the action and still push an enemy off a ledge with the bonus action. Source: bg3.wiki, Actions.

Movement: the resource that players forget

Movement is a resource with a number, not a free extra. Your base speed depends on your race. Source: bg3.wiki, Movement speed.

  • Dwarves, halflings and gnomes move 7.5 m (25 ft) per turn.
  • Most other playable races move 9 m (30 ft) per turn.
  • Wood elves and wood half-elves move 10.5 m (35 ft) per turn.

Three rules change that number during a fight:

  • The Dash action doubles your movement speed for that turn. Source: bg3.wiki, Movement speed.
  • Difficult Terrain halves your movement speed. The wiki states: "requiring 2 m (7 ft) of movement to move 1 m (3 ft) through this type of area." Source: bg3.wiki, Difficult Terrain.
  • A creature with the Prone condition must spend half its movement speed to stand up. Source: bg3.wiki, Prone (Condition).

Warning: a knockdown costs you more than one turn of position. The Prone condition ends the turn immediately. On the next turn you also pay half of your movement speed to stand up. Source: bg3.wiki, Prone (Condition).

The reaction: your resource on the enemy turn

You hold 1 reaction per turn. It recharges at the start of your turn. You spend it in response to a trigger, and the trigger often happens on the turn of another creature. Source: bg3.wiki, Reaction.

Two reactions matter at level 2:

  • Opportunity Attack. The game describes it as "Automatically attack an enemy moving out of your reach." The Disengage action prevents it. Source: bg3.wiki, Opportunity Attack.
  • Shield. This level 1 spell costs a reaction and a level 1 spell slot. It raises your Armour Class by 5 and gives immunity to Magic Missile. The effects last until the start of your next turn. Source: bg3.wiki, Shield.

Set your reactions before the fight, not during it. The Reactions tab sits on the spellbook page of the character sheet, and the default hotkey is L. Each reaction has a checkbox for enabled or disabled, plus an "Ask" setting. The "Ask" setting prompts you for confirmation each time the reaction triggers. Source: bg3.wiki, Reactions.

Some class features use the reaction system but do not spend the Reaction resource. Sneak Attack, Divine Smite and Reckless Attack are examples. A prompt for one of those features does not cost you the Shield reaction later in the round. Source: bg3.wiki, Reactions.

The turn checklist

Use this checklist for one complete fight. Tick each resource as you spend it, then reset it for the next turn. The target is 4 of 4 on every turn.

Concentration: one spell and one saving throw

You can hold only one concentration spell at a time. A second concentration spell ends the first one. Source: bg3.wiki, Concentration.

Damage puts the spell at risk. On damage you roll a Constitution saving throw.

Concentration DC = half the damage taken, or 10, whichever is higher

A natural 1 on this saving throw almost always ends the concentration. The bonus does not save you on that face. Source: bg3.wiki, Concentration.

Your Sorcerer has an advantage over Gale here. The Sorcerer is proficient in Constitution saving throws and in Charisma saving throws. The Wizard is proficient in Intelligence and Wisdom, and not in Constitution. Source: bg3.wiki, Saving throw. Therefore your Sorcerer adds the proficiency bonus to every concentration saving throw, and Gale does not.

Example: your Sorcerer has Constitution 14, so the modifier is +2. The proficiency bonus at level 2 is +2. The concentration save bonus is +4. A goblin arrow deals 7 damage. Half of 7 is 3, so the DC is 10. You need 10 − 4 = 6 or higher. Faces 6 to 20 are 15 faces, so the chance is 75 percent. A hit for 24 damage gives DC 12. You need 8 or higher, which is 13 faces, so the chance is 65 percent.

Set the Constitution modifier to 0 and turn the proficiency off. This result is the Wizard case. Compare it with the Sorcerer case at the same damage. The gap is the reason to give the concentration spell to your Sorcerer and the damage spell to Gale.

What ends concentration without a saving throw

Damage gives you a saving throw. The following events give you nothing. Each one ends the spell immediately. Source: bg3.wiki, Concentration.

  • You cast a second concentration spell.
  • You receive the Downed, Banished, Sleeping, Incapacitated, Prone or Rage condition.
  • You dismiss the companion who holds the spell.
  • You press the Flee Combat button.
  • You cancel the spell with the x on the spell icon.

The Prone entry is the trap. A shove, a grease surface or a knockdown attack ends your Hold Person with no roll. Source: bg3.wiki, Grease. Stay off flammable and slippery ground while you concentrate.

Grease is your control option with no concentration cost. Use Grease when you already hold another spell.

Quickened Spell: a second spell in one turn

Quickened Spell is a Metamagic option. It costs 3 sorcery points. The wiki states the effect as "Spells that take an Action take a Bonus Action instead." It becomes available at Sorcerer level 3. Source: bg3.wiki, Metamagic.

This option turns your bonus action into a second spell. One example: cast Hold Person with the action, then cast a Quickened Scorching Ray with the bonus action.

A limit exists in tabletop 5e: after a spell with a bonus action, the action can only cast a cantrip. The bg3.wiki Metamagic page states no such limit for BG3. The page gives the cost and the effect and nothing more. Treat this point as unverified until you test it in the game. Report the result to me.

Practice: 4 of 4 for one fight

Do this task in your next fight. Open the Reactions tab (hotkey L) before combat starts. Set Shield to "Ask" on your Sorcerer. Then cast one concentration spell on turn 1 and keep it to the end of the fight. On every turn, spend the action, the bonus action and the movement, and end the turn with the reaction still available. Use the checklist above. Count the turns where you reached 4 of 4.
