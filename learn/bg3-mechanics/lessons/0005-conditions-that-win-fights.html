Conditions that win fights

A control spell does little damage. A control spell still ends a fight faster than a damage spell. It removes the turn of the enemy, and it changes the attack rolls of your party. This lesson shows you which condition to apply, what each condition does, and how to combine a condition with the attacks of your allies.

Why a condition beats damage

A damage spell removes hit points. A condition removes the whole turn of the enemy. Prone, Paralysed, Stunned, and Sleeping all stop the creature from taking actions, bonus actions, and reactions. Sources: bg3.wiki, Prone, Paralysed, Stunned, Sleeping.

The condition also pays a second time. Most of these conditions give advantage on attack rolls against the target. Lesson 1 gives the number: a flat 65 percent becomes 88 percent with advantage. See Lesson 1 and the d20 cheat sheet.

Two conditions pay a third time. They turn a close hit into an automatic critical hit. This lesson names them below.

One control spell that lands on one enemy for 3 turns removes 3 enemy turns and raises the hit chance of every ally attack for those 3 turns. No level 1 or level 2 damage spell gives that much value.

The conditions, and what each one does

Use the lookup below. Select a condition and read the four rows: what the creature cannot do, what attackers get, which saving throws it fails, and how the condition ends.

Three groups are worth memorising.

  • Automatic critical hit group. Paralysed and Sleeping. An attack against a Paralysed creature from within 3 m is always a critical hit. An attack against a Sleeping creature from within 1.5 m is a critical hit. Sources: bg3.wiki, Paralysed, Sleeping.
  • Advantage group. Prone, Restrained, Enwebbed, Stunned, and Blinded. Attack rolls against the target have advantage. Prone gives that advantage only from within 3 m. Sources: bg3.wiki, Restrained, Enwebbed, Blinded.
  • Weaken group. Frightened and Threatened. These conditions give no advantage to your attacks. They only reduce the attack rolls of the enemy. Sources: bg3.wiki, Frightened, Threatened.

Prone: the cheapest control

A Prone creature cannot move or take actions, bonus actions, or reactions. It has disadvantage on Strength and Dexterity saving throws. Attacks against it have advantage from within 3 m. It must spend half its movement speed to stand up. Source: bg3.wiki, Prone.

Warning: the 3 m limit is real. A ranged attack from far away against a Prone target gets no advantage. Send the melee ally, not the archer.

Prone usually lasts 2 turns. Grease, ice, and Throw give 1 turn. If a creature falls Prone on its own turn, the turn ends at once and the concentration of that creature breaks. Source: bg3.wiki, Prone.

The concentration rule makes Grease a good answer to an enemy caster. The enemy walks into the grease, fails the Dexterity saving throw, falls Prone, loses the turn, and loses the spell it holds.

Blinded and Threatened: two different penalties

A Blinded creature has disadvantage on attack rolls. The range of its attacks and spells drops to 3 m. Attack rolls against it have advantage. It cannot make opportunity attacks. Source: bg3.wiki, Blinded.

Threatened is the condition on you, not on the enemy. A hostile creature in melee range gives you disadvantage on ranged attack rolls. A separate source gives disadvantage on a ranged attack against a target within 3 m. Neither source affects a melee attack or a spell that uses a saving throw. Source: bg3.wiki, Threatened.

This is the reason your Sorcerer casts a saving throw spell when an enemy stands next to you. Ray of Frost needs an attack roll and suffers the disadvantage. A Dexterity saving throw spell does not.

Warning: Shove does not knock a creature Prone by itself in BG3. Shove pushes the target. A creature that falls at least 8 m from the push is knocked Prone with no saving throw. Source: bg3.wiki, Shove.

Shove is a bonus action. Your Sorcerer can therefore cast a spell and Shove in the same turn. Use Shove to push an enemy off a ledge, or to move a melee enemy away from Gale.

The worked example: Hold Person plus a melee ally

Your enemy is a humanoid cultist. Your Sorcerer has a Spell Save DC of 13. Follow this order.

  • Cast Hold Person on the cultist. The cultist rolls a Wisdom saving throw against DC 13. Source: bg3.wiki, Hold Person.
  • On a failure the cultist cannot move or act. Attacks against it succeed automatically. An attack from within 3 m is always a critical hit. Source: bg3.wiki, Hold Person.
  • Move Lae'zel into melee range and attack twice. Both attacks are critical hits. Both attacks roll double damage dice.
  • Keep the concentration on Hold Person. The cultist rolls a new Wisdom saving throw at the end of each of its turns.

Compare this result with a Magic Missile on the same turn. Magic Missile does about 10 damage and changes nothing else. Hold Person removes every turn of the cultist and doubles the damage dice of every melee attack against it.

A correction: many guides say that Hold Person applies the Paralysed condition. In BG3 the spell applies its own Hold Person condition. The effects are almost the same. The one difference is important: attacks against a Hold Person target succeed automatically, and attacks against a Paralysed target have advantage. Sources: bg3.wiki, Hold Person, Paralysed.

How to choose the spell

Choose by the saving throw of the target, not by the damage of the spell. Use this short rule set.

  • Against a heavy armoured melee enemy, use Grease or Web. Both use a Dexterity saving throw, and heavy armour usually means low Dexterity.
  • Against a low intelligence brute, use Tasha's Hideous Laughter or Hold Person. Both use a Wisdom saving throw.
  • Against an archer or a caster with a good Wisdom, use Blindness. It uses a Constitution saving throw, and the range drop to 3 m removes the whole ranged attack of the target.
  • Against a group of weak enemies at the start of a fight, use Sleep. It needs no saving throw.

Practice: one condition, then one melee attack

Do this in your next fight. Pick the enemy with the most dangerous attack. Apply one condition from the advantage group or the automatic critical hit group. Then move a melee ally to within 3 m and attack. Open the combat log and confirm two lines: the saving throw that the enemy failed, and the advantage or the critical hit on the attack of your ally.
