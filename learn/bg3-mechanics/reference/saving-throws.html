Saving throws

This sheet gives the Spell Save DC formula, the DC table by ability modifier and level, the chance that a spell lands, and the saves that each low-level spell targets. Print this sheet.

The two formulas

Spell Save DC = 8 + proficiency bonus + spellcasting ability modifier
target: d20 + ability modifier + proficiency bonus (if proficient)  ≥  DC → the target saves
  • The target succeeds when the result equals or exceeds the DC. wiki
  • A saving throw has no automatic success on a natural 20 and no automatic failure on a natural 1. The only exceptions are concentration saves and dialogue saves. wiki
  • A ray spell or a projectile spell rolls an attack against the Armour Class instead. An area spell or a point-target spell uses a saving throw. wiki
  • The Sorcerer casts with Charisma. The Wizard casts with Intelligence. wiki

Needed face → chance that the spell lands

Need = your Spell Save DC − the save bonus of the target. Land (flat) = 1 − (21 − need) ÷ 20. Land (target has disadvantage) = 1 − save².

Advantage and disadvantage on saves

  • Advantage and disadvantage apply to saving throws in the same way as to attack rolls. wiki
  • Multiple sources of the same kind do not stack. The game still rolls two dice.
  • Any advantage plus any disadvantage cancels to one flat die.
  • Heightened Spell costs 3 sorcery points and gives the target disadvantage on its first saving throw. The Sorcerer gains this option at class level 3. wiki

The Examine panel

  1. Hover over the creature.
  2. Press T.
  3. Read the six saving throw numbers, the resistances, and the current conditions.
  4. Choose the spell that targets the lowest number.

Sources: bg3.wiki, Abilities for the shortcut and the saving throws; Prima Games for the resistances and the conditions.

Warning: the save number can exceed the ability modifier, because a proficient creature adds its proficiency bonus. Read the save number itself, and use the ability modifier only as a fallback.
